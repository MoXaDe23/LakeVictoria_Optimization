Flood-risk diagnostics were evaluated for Lake Victoria (2001–2021) using multi-threshold exceedance metrics at 1135.35 m asl (relative 12.50 m), 1135.65 m asl (relative 12.80 m), 1135.85 m asl (relative 13.00 m). At the 1135.65 m asl threshold, observed lake levels exceeded the threshold on 452 days, while the baseline Agreed Curve policy exceeded on 0 days and the optimized rule exceeded on 0 days. In addition to exceedance frequency, flood severity (sum of meters above threshold across exceedance days) showed that the optimized policy produced a total severity of 0.00 m-days at 1135.65 m asl relative to 162.74 m-days under observed conditions. Across thresholds, the optimized policy demonstrates improved flood-risk shaping (reduced peak severity and altered exceedance structure) compared to observed operations, while avoiding the excessive long-term drawdown behavior exhibited by the baseline policy.
